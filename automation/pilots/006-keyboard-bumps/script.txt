The tiny bumps on F and J are enough to map an entire keyboard without looking at it.

In touch typing, your left index finger rests on F and your right index finger rests on J. Those two keys anchor the home row. When your fingertips feel the raised bars, the remaining fingers naturally line up over A, S, D and K, L, semicolon.

From those two tactile reference points, a practiced typist reaches every other key by distance and muscle memory, then returns home. That keeps the eyes on the screen instead of repeatedly searching the keyboard. The number five on many numeric keypads uses the same idea.

Two almost invisible ridges turn a flat grid of keys into something your hands can navigate by touch.
