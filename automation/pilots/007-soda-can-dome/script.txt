The bottom of a soda can curves inward because a flat base would be a weak pressure plate.

Carbon dioxide pushes outward on every surface inside the can. A broad flat bottom would flex and bulge unless manufacturers used thicker aluminum. Forming the base into a dome spreads the load through a curved surface, making it much harder to deform with the same thin sheet of metal.

An outer ring, called the foot, still gives the can a stable surface to stand on. Between that foot and the dome, engineers carefully tune the shape to resist buckling, impacts, and changes in pressure as the drink warms.

That small inward curve is why an extremely light container can safely hold a pressurized drink without needing a heavy bottom.
