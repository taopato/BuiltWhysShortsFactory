Those black dots around a windshield are not printed decoration. They are the fading edge of a ceramic band fused into the glass.

The solid black section hides the uneven bead of adhesive that bonds the windshield to the car. It also shields that adhesive from ultraviolet light, which can weaken it over time. But ending a heat-absorbing black band with one sharp line can create an obvious visual and temperature transition.

So manufacturers use a halftone pattern: large dots near the band, then smaller and more widely spaced dots toward the clear glass. The change looks smoother and helps manage the transition during manufacturing and in sunlight.

What looks like a graphic detail is really bonding protection, heat management, and visual camouflage baked into one border.
