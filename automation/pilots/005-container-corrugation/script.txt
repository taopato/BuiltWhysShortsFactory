A shipping container has thin steel walls, yet those walls survive ships, cranes, wind, and years of vibration. The folds are a big reason why.

A large flat sheet bends easily because there is little depth resisting the load. Pressing the steel into repeating ridges changes its cross-section. The corrugations act like many narrow ribs, making the panel much stiffer without requiring a heavy solid plate.

The container's corner posts carry most of the stacking load, but the corrugated sidewalls still brace the box, resist sideways forces, and help prevent the frame from racking out of square. Research on container-like modules shows corrugated panels can significantly increase structural rigidity.

So those waves are not styling. They turn a thin sheet into a lightweight structural skin.
