The grooves in escalator steps are not mainly there for grip. They help the moving staircase disappear safely into a stationary floor.

At the top and bottom, each step slides beneath a comb plate. The plate has rows of narrow teeth that mesh with the grooves, almost like two combs passing through each other. That close fit helps keep larger objects from being carried straight into the gap where the moving steps vanish.

The grooves also channel small debris and liquid instead of letting everything collect on one flat surface. Safety standards tightly control how the comb teeth engage with the step tread.

So that striped metal pattern is part of a mechanical handoff: moving stair, stationary landing, and a comb between them.
